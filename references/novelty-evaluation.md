# Novelty Evaluation

Use when the task asks whether work is novel, what the innovation is, or where a research contribution may exist.

## P-L-M-Δ requirement

Do not present a candidate as an innovation unless it has:

- **P — Closest direct prior work**
- **L — Specific limitation of P relevant to the target problem**
- **M — Proposed mechanism or structural change**
- **Δ — Specific new capability, result class, experiment, or falsifiable consequence**

If one element is unsupported, call it an unresolved idea, not an innovation claim.

Difference from the user's previous draft is not evidence of novelty over the literature.

## Adversarial prior-art search

Try to invalidate the candidate before promoting it:

- search synonyms and older terminology;
- search equivalent mathematical descriptions, not only method names;
- follow references and cited-by chains;
- inspect relevant authors' earlier and later work;
- search adjacent disciplines where the same mechanism may have another name;
- distinguish method transfer to a new dataset from mechanism-level contribution.

Absence of a retrieved paper is not evidence of absence of prior art.

## Low-value candidates

Do not present these as scientific novelty without an additional mechanism-level delta:

- swapping dataset, disease, organism, or benchmark;
- adding a fashionable model family;
- generic ensemble or hyperparameter changes;
- CI, testing, packaging, renaming, or directory cleanup;
- ordinary software performance optimization;
- pipelines assembled from standard components.

## Novelty Adversary

When independent review materially improves confidence, give a read-only Novelty Adversary the raw candidate, target problem, and source set/search permission. Withhold the main agent's novelty conclusion and confidence.

Treat adversarial output as a hypothesis until validated against primary sources.

Investigate only findings with:
- concrete claim/object;
- evidence pointer or reproducible search path;
- checkable failure mechanism;
- material consequence.

Vague suspicion does not create a new research branch.
